How it is computed

Estimate, not an SSS computation. You need at least 3 monthly contributions in the 12 months before the semester of your delivery or miscarriage. The semester and the MSCs that count depend on your delivery date, so confirm your exact figures in My.SSS.

What this page is actually dividing

The number at the top is not your salary and it is not your full leave pay. Republic Act No. 11210 sets the length of maternity leave. SSS pays a cash allowance from your monthly salary credits. This calculator follows the rule stored for that allowance: add the six highest credits, count each one only up to ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}, divide by {{ 180 }}, and multiply by the days of leave. The 180 is six blocks of 30 days. It is not the number of calendar days you will be away from work, and it does not change for a caesarean delivery.

The formula this calculator runs

Average daily salary credit = total of the six highest MSCs (each capped at ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}) ÷ 180
Benefit = average daily salary credit × days of leave

Credits above ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }} are the MPF, or WISP, part of your SSS contribution. They raise the contribution you and your employer pay. They do not raise this benefit. A member whose posted credit is ₱25,000 is counted here as ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}. The full SSS schedule, including that split, is on Rates & sources and in the SSS contribution guide.

SSS pays the allowance. If your employer is required to advance your pay for the leave and then seek reimbursement, the difference between this allowance and your actual salary is between you and your employer. This page does not compute that difference.

Who qualifies, and which months count

You need at least three monthly contributions in the 12 months before the semester of the delivery or miscarriage, and the six credits are the highest ones in that stretch. The semester is the two consecutive quarters ending in the quarter of the contingency, not the six months before the due date, so two nearby due dates can average different months. This calculator does not choose them. Confirm the window on the SSS maternity benefit page, then read which months were posted in My.SSS.

  • Live birth, normal or caesarean: {{ leaveTypes[0].value }} days in this calculator.
  • Live birth as a solo parent: {{ leaveTypes[1].value }} days, if SSS accepts that status.
  • Miscarriage or emergency termination of pregnancy: {{ leaveTypes[2].value }} days.
  • Separated, self-employed, or voluntary members notify SSS. Employed members notify the employer, who deals with SSS. This page does not state a filing deadline. Confirm the timing for your case.

A month your employer deducted and did not remit is not a posted contribution. The eligibility test and the average both follow the posted record. If a month is missing, the argument with payroll has to happen before the average will move.

How to check the figure in My.SSS

  1. Sign in to My.SSS and open the posted contribution list, not the loan screen and not a payslip PDF.
  2. Mark the semester of your delivery or miscarriage, then the 12 months before it. If you are unsure where the semester starts, stop and use the illustration SSS shows for your date rather than guessing the quarters.
  3. From those 12 months, take the six highest regular salary credits. Ignore the portion above ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}.
  4. Add them and divide by 180, or paste the total into “Total of 6 MSCs” above. The benefit should match this page to the centavo if the six numbers match.
  5. When you file, the claim screen is the award. If SSS drops a month or moves the semester, their total replaces this estimate. The reimbursement your employer receives can also differ from the allowance you were advanced.

Mistakes that change the result

  • Typing basic pay instead of the posted MSC. A ₱18,300 salary is not an ₱18,300 credit. Contributions round to a ₱500 step. This benefit then caps that credit at ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}.
  • Adding the MPF line on top. It is already the part of the MSC above ₱{{ mscCap | number: '1.0-0' }}, and this formula throws that part away.
  • Dividing by 365, or by the number of leave days, instead of 180. The 105, 120 or 60 multiplies the daily credit. It is not the divisor.
  • Expecting a higher allowance for a caesarean. The day count in this calculator does not do that.
  • Counting a separated or voluntary member’s unposted months because contributions were “supposed” to be paid. Only posted months enter the total.
