How it is computed

Estimate. The tax on any excess depends on your total income for the year, so the rate you enter is an assumption. Your employer's payroll will apply the exact amount.

Final pay uses the basic you actually earned

Presidential Decree No. 851 is the 13th-month mandate for rank-and-file employees in the private sector who worked at least one month in the calendar year. This calculator does not decide who is rank-and-file. It divides basic pay earned by 12. That is the whole formula. Overtime, night differential, holiday premium, COLA, and converted leave are not in the boxes, and they should not be typed in as if they were basic pay.

Someone still employed is generally paid by 24 December. Someone who already resigned is not waiting for that date in order to have earned the pro-rated amount. It is part of pay for work already done. Whether the employer puts it in the final-pay release or in the December run is a practice to confirm with them. This page does not invent a number of days for that release.

Three amounts, all from the divide-by-12 function

A full year at ₱{{ fullYear.basic | number: '1.0-0' }} with no unpaid basic is ₱{{ fullYear.basic * fullYear.months | number: '1.0-0' }} ÷ 12 = ₱{{ fullYear.gross | number: '1.2-2' }}. The entire amount is inside the ₱{{ exemptionCap | number: '1.0-0' }} cap, so the taxable excess is ₱{{ fullYear.taxable | number: '1.2-2' }}.

Hired at the start of August and still there in December is five months at ₱{{ hired.basic | number: '1.0-0' }}, or ₱125,000 of basic. Divided by 12 that is ₱{{ hired.gross | number: '1.2-2' }}, also fully inside the cap. Resigning at the end of those five months does not change the division. It changes when you ask for the cheque.

A ₱40,000 basic with one month cut to ₱20,000 by unpaid absence is ₱{{ absence.earned | number: '1.0-0' }} of basic for the year, and the 13th month is ₱{{ absence.gross | number: '1.2-2' }}. The unpaid days matter only because they reduced basic pay. A paid vacation does not reduce it. Do not subtract “days of leave” unless those days were unpaid.

The ₱{{ exemptionCap | number: '1.0-0' }} bucket is shared

Republic Act No. 10963, the TRAIN law, exempts 13th-month pay and other benefits up to a combined ₱{{ exemptionCap | number: '1.0-0' }}. Christmas bonus and productivity incentives sit in the same bucket. The form’s “other benefits” box is how you tell the calculator that some of the bucket is already used.

₱{{ sharedCap.other | number: '1.0-0' }} of other benefits leaves ₱{{ sharedCap.left | number: '1.0-0' }} of room. An ₱{{ sharedCap.gross | number: '1.0-0' }} 13th-month payment then has ₱{{ sharedCap.nonTaxable | number: '1.2-2' }} exempt and ₱{{ sharedCap.taxable | number: '1.2-2' }} taxable. The tax is not looked up in a bracket. You type a rate. At 20% the tax is ₱{{ sharedCap.tax | number: '1.2-2' }} and the amount in hand is ₱{{ sharedCap.net | number: '1.2-2' }}. That 20% is a placeholder you can change. It is not this site’s opinion of your bracket.

A ₱{{ aboveCap.gross | number: '1.0-0' }} 13th-month payment and no other benefits leaves ₱{{ aboveCap.nonTaxable | number: '1.2-2' }} exempt and ₱{{ aboveCap.taxable | number: '1.2-2' }} taxable. At a typed rate of 25% the tax is ₱{{ aboveCap.tax | number: '1.2-2' }} and you receive ₱{{ aboveCap.net | number: '1.2-2' }}. Payroll that annualizes your tax for the year can withhold a different amount, because it can see the rest of your compensation and this form cannot. The excess is what is taxable. The rate on the excess is the open question.

Managers and supervisors are outside the decree’s mandatory coverage. Many employers pay them anyway, as policy. If yours does, the same divide-by-12 arithmetic applies to the basic they include. If yours does not, this calculator will still produce a number. The number is not a claim you can file.
