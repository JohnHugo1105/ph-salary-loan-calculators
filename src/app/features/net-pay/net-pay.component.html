Assumes one employer, no other income, a private-sector employee, and contributions on basic salary only. Your employer's payroll may round or treat allowances differently, so compare with your payslip.

Read the payslip from the basic line down

A payslip is a stack of lines, and this calculator only rebuilds some of them. Start at basic pay. Under it, the employee shares of SSS, MPF/WISP, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG come off. What is left is the taxable pay for that period, and the withholding tax is computed on that remainder, not on the basic. Take-home pay is basic, plus any taxable allowance, overtime, or holiday premium you added, minus those contributions and minus the tax.

The rates and ceilings live on Rates & sources. The employer’s half of each contribution is not a payslip deduction. It is on the contributions calculator. 13th-month pay is a separate cheque, with its own ₱{{ 90000 | number: '1.0-0' }} rule, on the 13th month page.

A ₱{{ example.salary | number: '1.0-0' }} monthly payslip, with nothing else on it

Basic pay is ₱{{ example.salary | number: '1.0-0' }}. The employee SSS line is ₱{{ example.sssOnly | number: '1.2-2' }} and the MPF/WISP line is ₱{{ example.mpf | number: '1.2-2' }}. They are one contribution split across two labels. PhilHealth is ₱{{ example.philhealth | number: '1.2-2' }} and Pag-IBIG is ₱{{ example.pagibig | number: '1.2-2' }}. Contributions together are ₱{{ example.govt | number: '1.2-2' }}.

Taxable pay is ₱{{ example.salary | number: '1.0-0' }} − ₱{{ example.govt | number: '1.2-2' }} = ₱{{ example.taxable | number: '1.2-2' }}. That amount sits in the monthly withholding bracket that starts at ₱20,833: 15% of the excess, which is ₱{{ example.tax | number: '1.2-2' }}. Take-home pay is ₱{{ example.net | number: '1.2-2' }}. If your payslip’s SSS line is not ₱{{ example.sss | number: '1.2-2' }} combined, the basic used for contributions is not ₱{{ example.salary | number: '1.0-0' }}, or a month was posted on a different salary credit. The tax will then be off by the same reason. Do not “fix” the tax first.

The same salary, paid twice a month

Each contribution is computed on the monthly basic and then halved, with each half rounded to the centavo. One payday’s basic is ₱{{ semi.basic | number: '1.2-2' }}. SSS is ₱{{ semi.sss | number: '1.2-2' }}, MPF is ₱{{ semi.mpf | number: '1.2-2' }}, PhilHealth is ₱{{ semi.philhealth | number: '1.2-2' }}, and Pag-IBIG is ₱{{ semi.pagibig | number: '1.2-2' }}. Contributions on that payday are ₱{{ semi.govt | number: '1.2-2' }}.

Taxable pay for the payday is ₱{{ semi.taxable | number: '1.2-2' }}. The semi-monthly table, not half of the monthly tax, produces ₱{{ semi.tax | number: '1.2-2' }}. Take-home on that payday is ₱{{ semi.net | number: '1.2-2' }}. Two paydays are not equal to the monthly take-home above, because the two BIR tables do not halve. Overtime and holiday pay are not halved either: this calculator puts them on the payday you type them into, on top of that period’s basic.

Working backwards from the amount you want to keep

Reverse mode ignores allowances, overtime, and holiday pay. It searches for a monthly basic whose take-home, after the same contributions and the same tax table, lands within about a peso of your target. Contribution brackets move in steps, so some targets have no exact basic.

A target of ₱25,000 a month resolves to a basic of ₱{{ reverseExample.monthlyBasic | number: '1.2-2' }}. Running that basic forward leaves ₱{{ reverseExample.netPay | number: '1.2-2' }}. The contributions on that basic are ₱{{ reverseExample.totalContributions | number: '1.2-2' }} and the monthly tax is ₱{{ reverseExample.taxPerPeriod | number: '1.2-2' }}. If you are negotiating an offer, quote the basic, not the target. The employer’s payroll will not search for your net. It will start from the basic in the contract and may also deduct a loan or an absence this search never saw.

Lines that should not be fed in as taxable pay

De minimis benefits under BIR Revenue Regulations No. 29-2025 stay off the taxable-allowance box while they are inside the ceiling:

  • Rice subsidy: Up to ₱{{ deMinimis.riceSubsidyMonthly | number: '1.0-0' }} per month.
  • Uniform and clothing allowance: Up to ₱{{ deMinimis.clothingAllowanceAnnual | number: '1.0-0' }} per year.
  • Medical cash for dependents: Up to ₱{{ deMinimis.medicalCashDependentsPerSemester | number: '1.0-0' }} per semester.
  • Laundry allowance: Up to ₱{{ deMinimis.laundryAllowanceMonthly | number: '1.0-0' }} per month.
  • Monetized private-sector vacation leave: Up to {{ deMinimis.monetizedVacationLeaveDays }} days.

The amount above a ceiling is taxable. A rice subsidy of ₱3,000 is not “₱3,000 taxable” and it is not “all exempt”. The first ₱{{ deMinimis.riceSubsidyMonthly | number: '1.0-0' }} stays out, and the rest goes in the allowance box.

Company loans, cash advances, tardiness, and absences reduce the cash you receive and, if they reduce basic pay, they can also change the contribution base. This form has no field for them. Compare the basic first. If the basic matches and the net does not, the missing line is one of those adjustments, not a wrong tax table.
