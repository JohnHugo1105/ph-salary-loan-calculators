Two ways to turn one rate into a monthly payment

This calculator does only the second of them. A diminishing balance charges each month’s interest on whatever principal is still unpaid. The payment stays the same, so as the balance falls, more of each payment is principal. An add-on rate does the opposite kind of arithmetic: it multiplies the original principal by the stated annual rate and by the number of years, adds that interest to the principal, and divides by the number of months. The interest never shrinks, because it was computed once, on the amount you borrowed on day one.

The comparison below uses {{ illustrationRate }}%. That number is the five-year rate stored for our Pag-IBIG housing calculator. It is not a quote from a commercial bank, and on Rates & sources it is marked for you to confirm with Pag-IBIG. No other rate is invented here to stand in for BDO, BPI, or anyone else. If you have a bank’s disclosure, type that rate into the form. Do not assume {{ illustrationRate }}% is what they will charge.

₱1,000,000 at {{ illustrationRate }}% for five years

Add-on interest is ₱1,000,000 × {{ illustrationRate }}% × 5 = ₱{{ add5.interest | number: '1.0-0' }}. Principal plus interest is ₱{{ add5.total | number: '1.0-0' }}. Split over {{ add5.months }} months, the payment is ₱{{ add5.payment | number: '1.2-2' }}.

The diminishing-balance function on the same inputs pays ₱{{ dim5.payment | number: '1.2-2' }} a month. Total interest is ₱{{ dim5.totalInterest | number: '1.2-2' }}, not ₱{{ add5.interest | number: '1.0-0' }}. The first month of that schedule charges ₱{{ dim5.schedule[0].interest | number: '1.2-2' }} of interest and sends ₱{{ dim5.schedule[0].principal | number: '1.2-2' }} to principal. The last month charges ₱{{ dim5.schedule[59].interest | number: '1.2-2' }} of interest and ₱{{ dim5.schedule[59].principal | number: '1.2-2' }} of principal. Same stated rate, smaller interest bill, because the lender stopped charging interest on principal you had already returned.

The same ₱1,000,000 stretched to 20 years

Add-on interest becomes ₱1,000,000 × {{ illustrationRate }}% × 20 = ₱{{ add20.interest | number: '1.0-0' }}, and the payment is ₱{{ add20.payment | number: '1.2-2' }}. You would hand over more in interest than you borrowed, and the method never gives you credit for paying the loan down.

Diminishing balance at the same rate pays ₱{{ dim20.payment | number: '1.2-2' }} a month. Interest over 240 months is ₱{{ dim20.totalInterest | number: '1.2-2' }}. The payment is lower than the five-year diminishing payment because the principal is sliced thinner, and the interest is higher than the five-year interest because the balance sits there longer. That trade-off is the term decision. It is not a reason to prefer an add-on contract that quotes a lower “rate per month” without saying which method it uses. Ask which method before you compare two offers. A lower add-on rate can still cost more than a higher diminishing rate.

What a fixing period does to this schedule

The schedule on this page holds the rate you typed until the last row. A housing loan often promises the rate only for a fixing period, then recomputes the payment on the balance that is left. The rows after that month are a picture of the old rate, not a forecast. Our Pag-IBIG page says this in more detail and keeps its own rate table. Neither page pulls a live bank board.

A payment you make on top of the amortization, if the contract applies it to principal, shortens the schedule this table cannot redraw for you. The contract also decides whether that extra payment is allowed without a fee. Read that clause. The calculator has no field for it.
