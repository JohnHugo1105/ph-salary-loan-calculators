In 2026 the SSS contribution is {{ r.total }} of your Monthly Salary Credit (MSC). You pay {{ r.employee }} and your employer pays {{ r.employer }}, plus a small Employees’ Compensation (EC) premium that only the employer pays. This is the final step of the rate increases under the Social Security Act of 2018 (RA 11199), in effect since January 2025.

The short version

  1. Round your monthly salary to the nearest {{ r.step }} to get your MSC (minimum {{ r.mscMin }}, maximum {{ r.mscMax }}).
  2. Multiply the MSC by {{ r.employee }} for your share and by {{ r.employer }} for your employer’s share.
  3. The part computed on the MSC above {{ r.regularMax }} is your MPF (WISP) savings; the rest is regular SSS.
  4. Your employer adds an EC premium: {{ r.ecLow }} if the MSC is below {{ r.ecThreshold }}, otherwise {{ r.ecHigh }}.

Step 1: Find your Monthly Salary Credit

SSS does not charge a percentage of your exact salary. It first converts your salary into a Monthly Salary Credit: salaries are grouped into brackets {{ r.step }} wide, and each bracket has one MSC. In practice that means rounding to the nearest {{ r.step }}: a salary within {{ r.half }} of an MSC uses that MSC.

  • Below the lowest bracket, the MSC is {{ r.mscMin }}.
  • Above the highest bracket, the MSC stays at {{ r.mscMax }}, so contributions stop growing past that point.

Example: a monthly salary of {{ walk.salary }} falls in the bracket whose MSC is {{ walk.msc }}.

Step 2: Apply the {{ r.total }} rate

Multiply the MSC by the rate for each side. With an MSC of {{ walk.msc }}, you pay {{ r.employee }} ({{ walk.employee }}) and your employer pays {{ r.employer }} ({{ walk.employerSss }} before EC). Your employer deducts only your share from your pay; their share is an extra cost to them.

Step 3: Split regular SSS and MPF

The Mandatory Provident Fund (MPF, also called the Workers’ Investment and Savings Program or WISP) is a savings account inside SSS. It receives the part of the contribution computed on the MSC above {{ r.regularMax }}. Everything on the first {{ r.regularMax }} goes to the regular SSS program that pays for pension, sickness, maternity and other benefits.

An MSC of {{ walk.msc }} is below {{ r.regularMax }}, so the MPF part is {{ walk.employeeMpf }}. See what MPF on your payslip means and how it compares with Pag-IBIG.

Step 4: Add the EC premium (employer only)

Employees’ Compensation covers work-related injury and illness. The employer pays {{ r.ecLow }} a month when the MSC is below {{ r.ecThreshold }} and {{ r.ecHigh }} from {{ r.ecThreshold }} up. It is never deducted from your salary. For an MSC of {{ walk.msc }} it is {{ walk.ec }}, so the employer’s total is {{ walk.employer }}.

Self-employed and voluntary members

Without an employer to share the cost, self-employed and voluntary members pay both shares, so the full {{ r.total }} of the MSC they declare. The same MSC brackets and the same {{ r.regularMax }} split between regular SSS and MPF apply. Check the minimum MSC for your membership type with SSS before you pay.

How to check what was actually posted

Log in to My.SSS on the SSS website or the SSS mobile app and open your contribution records. If a month is missing or lower than expected, ask your HR or payroll team first: employers remit on your behalf, and late or short remittances can affect your loan and benefit eligibility.

Other SSS measures, only where SSS has published them

These are not contribution rates, and this calculator does not apply them. Each one is limited to what SSS wrote.

Pension increase brought forward to June 2026

SSS’s 1 June 2026 announcement moved the second tranche of the Pension Reform Program from September 2026 to June for pensioners as of 31 May 2026. Retirement and disability pensions increase by 10%. Death and survivor pensions increase by 5%. A contingency that falls from 1 June through 31 August 2026 is paid the increase from 1 September 2026, not from June. SSS described the June-to-August release as about ₱6 billion. That figure is theirs, not a calculation on this site.

Emergency loan under Circular No. 2026-003

The emergency loan program now asks for 18 posted monthly contributions, six of them in the last 12 months, down from the old 36-month line. SSS’s page caps the loan at ₱20,000: up to half the average salary credit with 18 to 35 contributions, and up to the full average with 36 or more. The stated rate is 7% a year with no service fee, or 10% if a penalty condonation was used in the past five years, plus a six-month moratorium. The circular says moratorium interest is spread across the later payments and does not itself earn interest. Our loan calculator compounds that interest instead, and it has no 10% emergency option. Use it to see the loan amount, then read the disclosure in My.SSS for the payment.

Penalty condonation is the older Conso Loan, still open

This is not a new 2026 waiver. Circular No. 2022-022 is the Consolidated Loan Program with Condonation of Penalty. SSS’s Conso Loan page, which its 16 April 2026 note was still pointing to, says penalties are waived as principal and interest are settled: in one payment, or over as long as 60 months after a down payment of at least 10%. A balance left at the end of the term puts the uncondoned penalty back. Filing the application does not, by itself, wipe the penalty.

Facial authentication is not for every pensioner

SSS’s 11 March 2026 release offers Annual Confirmation of Pensioners by facial authentication with a liveness check, without a My.SSS login, for pensioners registered in PhilSys. The groups named are retirement pensioners aged 80 and above who live in the Philippines, retirement pensioners living abroad, and survivorship pensioners. Other pensioners were not included in that announcement. Branch visits and video appointments were left in place. The steps are on SSS’s ACOP facial-authentication page.
