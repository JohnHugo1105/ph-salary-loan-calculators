The short answer

MPF stands for the SSS Mandatory Provident Fund, also called the Workers’ Investment and Savings Program (WISP). The MPF line on your payslip is the part of your SSS contribution computed on your Monthly Salary Credit (MSC) above {{ r.regularMax }}. It goes into your own retirement savings account inside SSS, separate from the regular SSS program that pays pensions and other benefits.

  • It is part of your SSS deduction, not an extra charge.
  • It only appears when your MSC is above {{ r.regularMax }}, from a monthly salary of about {{ r.mpfFrom }}.
  • It is not Pag-IBIG. Pag-IBIG (HDMF) has its own line on your payslip.

Example: the SSS and MPF lines on a {{ example.salary }} salary

A {{ example.salary }} monthly salary has an MSC of {{ example.salary }}. SSS charges you {{ r.sssEmployee }} of it, {{ example.total }}, and splits that in two:

  • SSS {{ example.sss }}: {{ r.sssEmployee }} of the first {{ r.regularMax }}, for the regular SSS program.
  • MPF {{ example.mpf }}: {{ r.sssEmployee }} of the part above {{ r.regularMax }}, for your MPF savings.

Your employer adds {{ r.sssEmployer }} on the same split, so more of your MPF savings come from them than from you. On a {{ low.salary }} salary the MPF is {{ low.mpf }}, and at the MSC cap of {{ r.mscMax }} it reaches {{ max.mpf }} a month. The SSS contribution guide walks through the MSC brackets step by step.

MPF vs Pag-IBIG at a glance

SSS MPF (WISP) Pag-IBIG (HDMF)
Run by Social Security System Home Development Mutual Fund
You pay {{ r.sssEmployee }} of the MSC above {{ r.regularMax }} {{ r.pagibigEmployee }} of your salary, counted up to {{ r.pagibigCap }}
Your employer pays {{ r.sssEmployer }} of the MSC above {{ r.regularMax }} {{ r.pagibigEmployer }} of your salary, counted up to {{ r.pagibigCap }}
Most you pay a month {{ max.mpf }} {{ max.pagibig }}
Who has it Members with an MSC above {{ r.regularMax }} Every member
What it is for Retirement savings on top of your SSS pension Savings, plus access to housing and short-term loans

Which is “better”?

You don’t choose between them: both are mandatory, and they do different jobs. Pag-IBIG gives you access to housing and short-term loans, and it runs the voluntary MP2 savings program. The MPF is a retirement top-up under SSS for higher earners. What matters is that your employer deducts and remits both correctly.
