Pag-IBIG (HDMF) and the SSS Mandatory Provident Fund (MPF) are both savings components attached to employment, but they serve different roles. This guide clarifies the differences so you can read your payslip with confidence.

Pag-IBIG basics

Pag-IBIG contributions are based on a capped monthly fund salary (₱10,000 since February 2024). Employees contribute 1% if the monthly salary is ₱1,500 or below, otherwise 2% (up to ₱200 a month). Employers contribute 2%. Funds can be used for loans and long-term savings programs.

MPF basics

The SSS MPF (also called WISP) is the provident savings part of your SSS contribution. SSS collects 15% of your Monthly Salary Credit (MSC, up to ₱35,000). The regular pension fund receives the part on the MSC up to ₱20,000, and the MPF receives the part on the MSC above ₱20,000: 5% from the employee and 10% from the employer. If the MSC is ₱20,000 or below, the MPF is zero. It is not charged in addition to SSS: it is included in the SSS total.

What you’ll notice on payslips

  • Pag-IBIG employee share is small due to the ₱10,000 cap (at most ₱200), but appears monthly.
  • MPF may be zero at lower salaries and increases with higher MSCs.
  • Employers shoulder a larger share for both, especially MPF.

Which is “better”?

They’re complementary. Pag-IBIG provides accessible housing/short-term loans and savings options. MPF is a retirement-oriented top-up under SSS. Both contribute to long-term financial security. Your employer’s adherence to current rules is what matters for actual deductions.
