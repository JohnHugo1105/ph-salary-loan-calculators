Most housing, car, and salary loans in the Philippines use fixed monthly amortization. Each payment has two parts: interest (cost of borrowing) and principal (the amount that reduces your outstanding balance). Over time, the interest portion declines while the principal portion grows.

The payment formula

Monthly payment depends on loan amount, term (months), and monthly interest rate. With a fixed-rate loan, the payment is constant and is computed with the standard annuity formula used by banks, Pag-IBIG and our calculators:

Monthly amortization

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
  • M: the fixed monthly payment
  • P: the amount borrowed (principal)
  • r: the monthly interest rate, which is the annual rate ÷ 12
  • n: the number of monthly payments (years × 12)

Add-on rate vs diminishing balance

Some car loans, appliance plans and salary loans quote an add-on rate. Interest is charged on the original amount for the whole term, even though you repay part of it every month. The same quoted rate therefore costs far more than a diminishing-balance loan.

  • {{ addOn.amount }} for {{ addOn.years }} years at {{ addOn.rate }} add-on: interest is {{ addOn.amount }} × {{ addOn.rate }} × {{ addOn.years }} = {{ addOn.interest }}, so you pay {{ addOn.payment }} a month.
  • The same loan at {{ addOn.rate }} on a diminishing balance: {{ addOn.diminishingPayment }} a month and {{ addOn.diminishingInterest }} in total interest.
  • The add-on loan is equivalent to about {{ addOn.equivalent }} a year on a diminishing balance.

When a lender quotes an add-on or “flat” rate, ask for the effective annual rate or compare the monthly payments directly in the loan amortization calculator.

Practical tips

  • Compare APR across lenders, not just monthly payments.
  • Check for prepayment penalties and fees.
  • Run “what-if” scenarios: longer term lowers payment but increases total interest.
  • For loans that reprice, plan for the payment to change at each repricing date.
