How Pag-IBIG MP2 savings work

The Modified Pag-IBIG 2 (MP2) program is a voluntary savings account for Pag-IBIG members with a 5-year term. It pays a higher dividend than regular Pag-IBIG savings, the savings are government-guaranteed, and the dividends are tax-free. That is why many Filipinos, at home and abroad, use it in place of a bank time deposit.

MP2 is not the same as the MPF line on your payslip, which is retirement savings under SSS. See what MPF on your payslip means.

How MP2 dividends are computed

Dividend for the year = average monthly balance × declared dividend rate

Pag-IBIG declares the rate once a year, after the year ends, and applies it to your average balance over that year. Money you add during the year only counts for the months it was in the fund, so monthly savings earn about half the rate in the year you put them in. Savings carried over from earlier years earn the full rate.

Example: {{ walk.monthly }} a month at {{ rate }}, dividends reinvested

  • Year 1: you save {{ walk.yearSaved }}. Spread across the year, your average balance is about {{ walk.average }}, so the dividend is about {{ walk.average }} × {{ rate }} = {{ walk.year1 }}.
  • Year 2: the {{ walk.carried }} you ended year 1 with earns the full rate, {{ walk.carriedDividend }}. This year’s {{ walk.yearSaved }} earns about {{ walk.year1 }} again, so the dividend is {{ walk.year2 }}.
  • Years 3 to 5 repeat this, with a bigger carried balance each year.

The calculator assumes you save evenly through the year. Pag-IBIG uses the dates your savings are actually posted, so your dividends will differ a little: paying earlier in the year, or as a lump sum at the start, earns more.
